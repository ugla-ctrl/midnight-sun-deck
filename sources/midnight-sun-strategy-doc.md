**MIDNIGHT SUN**

**Reykjavík · 9–11 July 2027 · Hljómskálagarðurinn** *IMXP + Secret Solstice · Draft for internal discussion, 19 Aug 2026*

## 1. The one-line concept

**Iceland Eclipse took 3,333 people to the edge of the world for two minutes of darkness. Midnight Sun brings three to four times that many into the middle of Reykjavík for three days where the darkness never comes at all.**

Iceland Eclipse was an expedition. Midnight Sun is a city takeover. That inversion, remote to urban, scarcity to accessibility, two minutes of night to seventy-two hours of light, is the strategic logic of the follow-up, and it is what turns a once-in-a-century astronomical event into a repeatable annual business.

## 2. Why this event, why now

### 2.1 We just proved the demand and then turned most of it away

[FACT] Iceland Eclipse (11–15 Aug 2026) sold 3,333 passes and drew guests from 75+ countries with 287 artists and speakers. [FACT] Snæfellsnes forced that cap: Hellissandur is a village of 360 people. [FACT] Reykjavík hotel rates during the eclipse ran **more than 140% above baseline**, with average nightly rates above $1,000, and Airbnb rates inside the path of totality up 53.7%.

The people who couldn't get accommodation, couldn't take a week off for a remote site, or simply couldn't get a pass, plus the eclipse campaign's audience, are the founding market for Midnight Sun. [ASSUMPTION] Conversion needs modelling off the actual eclipse purchase funnel before we set a capacity target. This is the number the whole plan hangs on and we do not have it yet.

### 2.2 Reykjavík removes the constraint that capped the eclipse

|  | **Iceland Eclipse 2026** | **Midnight Sun 2027** |
|---|---|---|
| Site | Hellissandur, pop. 360 | Reykjavík, pop. ~140,000 |
| Beds within 30 min | Effectively none | ~5,620 hotel rooms + ~4,185 short-term rentals |
| Airport transfer | ~2.5h drive from KEF | ~50 min from KEF |
| Local audience | Marginal | 140k in the city, 250k+ in the capital region |
| Weather contingency | Open land, no shelter | Indoor venues on every side of the park |
| Capacity ceiling set by | Accommodation | Permit and turf |

### 2.3 July is the cleanest slot in the Icelandic calendar, and it just got cleaner

[FACT] August in Iceland is fully occupied: Þjóðhátíð (~15,000, Vestmannaeyjar) and the Verslunarmannahelgi national travel weekend at the start, Reykjavík Pride (80,000+ at peak, terminating *in our park*) in the second week, Menningarnótt (~100,000, with a stage in our park) around the 21st.

[FACT] **Mid-July is now genuinely vacant.** LungA in Seyðisfjörður, the one festival that occupied the slot, ran its **final edition in 2024** and has not returned. There is no longer a domestic clash for Icelandic artists, crew, gear or press attention in early-to-mid July. This is a real and recent opening.

## 3. The creative spine: a festival with no night

### 3.1 The astronomy

[FACT] On 9–11 July in Reykjavík the sun rises ~03:25–03:30 and sets ~23:33–23:40, roughly **20 hours of daylight**. Solar midnight is ~01:33 and the sun's lowest point is only about **3.5° below the horizon**, so there is no astronomical night and no nautical night on these dates; the darkest moment of the weekend is civil twilight. [FACT] *(Precision matters if we ever move the date: from ~25 July the sun does begin dipping into nautical twilight. The "no night at all" claim is true for 1–24 July and not beyond it.)*

[FACT] The consequence is better than we first thought. The sun sits inside the golden-hour band (−4° to +6°) from about **21:55 straight through to about 05:10**, roughly **seven hours of low, raking light**, not the three we assumed in v1. At 21:00 the sun is still ~11° up: bright, high, ordinary. The magic starts around 22:00 and does not stop until well after most festivals have gone home.

Two design consequences:

1. **Seven hours of golden hour is the most valuable production asset the event owns, and it is free.**
2. **Nothing in the standard festival playbook works.** No lasers, no darkness reveal, no LED-as-sole-light-source, no headliner-under-the-stars. Every production convention has to be rebuilt, which is exactly why it will look like nothing else on the circuit.

### 3.2 Dayparts as the programming architecture

We structure by quality of light rather than by stage. Working names:

| **Daypart** | **Hours** | **Light** | **Character** | **Where** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **OPEN** | 12:00–17:00 | High sun | Art, talks, wellness, family, markets, emerging Icelandic acts | Park |
| **HIGH** | 17:00–22:00 | Still high | Main-stage build, big crossover bookings | Park |
| **LOW SUN** | 22:00–00:00 | **Golden hour begins ~21:55** | **The signature slot.** Headliners, live acts, choral/orchestral spectacle, the image the festival is remembered by | Park |
| **WHITE NIGHT** | 00:00–03:00 | Civil twilight, low point 01:33 | Club and late programming across downtown | City venues |
| **FIRST LIGHT** | 03:00–06:00 | **Still golden until ~05:10** | Sunrise sets, pools, cold water, ambient, ceremony | Pools, harbour, select venues |

The best light of the weekend brackets the WHITE NIGHT handoff on *both* sides. That is a genuinely unusual dramaturgy and it should drive the whole production design.

## 4. The regulatory reality (read this before designing anything)

*(Full detail lives in the original doc; summarized here since the deck deliberately omits most of this section, it's internal planning risk, not investor-facing copy.)*

Key constraints: event curfew (01:00, extended to 03:00 Fri/Sat only for non-public events per Art. 14, a public festival is excluded), night noise capped at 40 dB(A) 23:00–07:00 at the façade, event noise capped at 95/110/130 dB, organiser bears full policing cost past 23:30, three separate permitting authorities (Reykjavíkurborg, sýslumaður's *tækifærisleyfi* which actually sets the hours, and the Chief of Police), and an unresolved open question about whether the park is inside the extended alcohol-hours zone (22:00 vs 23:30 outdoor cutoff) — this is **Open Decision #1** in the doc and is not yet answered.

## 5. The venue

### 5.1 Hljómskálagarðurinn

[FACT] At the south end of Tjörnin, wrapping Suðurtjörn and Þorfinnstjörn, bounded roughly by Fríkirkjuvegur/Sóleyjargata, Bjarkargata, Skothúsvegur and Hringbraut. The Hljómskálinn bandstand dates to 1923.

[FACT] **Reykjavík City built a purpose-designed event lawn ("viðburðaflöt") here in 2023**, roughly ISK 120–130m, with sports-grade turf, sub-surface irrigation, drainage to Þorfinnstjörn, a grágrýti retaining wall with integrated lighting, and paved paths. The city's stated goal was to "make the garden better equipped to receive crowds on major celebration days."

[FACT] **It was built because the 2018 World Cup fan zone destroyed the previous turf**, dust, then mud for the rest of the summer. **Documented evidence that the city is sensitive about this specific lawn, and that grass will not survive three days of festival unprotected.** A funded turf-protection and restoration bond belongs in our *first* conversation with the city, not our last.

[FACT] Precedent at the venue: the Reykjavík Pride parade terminates here for its outdoor concert (city estimated 80,000+ at peak in 2025); 17 June national day; a secondary Menningarnótt music programme; the 2018 fan zone.

[FACT] **No published capacity figure exists** for the park or the event lawn.

[ASSUMPTION] Working range of **6,000–10,000 per day ticketed**, pending survey and city consultation. [DECISION] Site survey from Borgarvefsjá, egress modelling, and a direct capacity conversation with city and police.

### 5.3 Weather

[FACT] July in Reykjavík: average high ~13°C, low ~9°C, 31–34% chance of a wet day, mean wind ~13 km/h predominantly southerly, overcast or mostly cloudy ~62% of the time. Wind loading on temporary structures is the real production risk, not rain and not heat.

## 6. Programming architecture

Four pillars inherited from Iceland Eclipse, re-weighted for an urban, higher-volume, half-local audience: **DANCE** (the anchor, LOW SUN + WHITE NIGHT), **EXPLORE** (the second business, §7), **LEARN** (compressed to ~20–30 curated sessions in real venues, not a summit), **CONNECT** (wellness anchored in the city's pools, a FIRST LIGHT pool programme at 04:00).

[DECISION] Proposal: **≥40% of DANCE slots Icelandic, and at least one Icelandic act at the top of each day.** This is a proposal in the doc, not yet a confirmed commitment.

## 7. The travel business

### 7.1 Flights

[FACT] Keflavík handled ~8.3M passengers in 2024 and ~8.4M in 2025. Summer 2026: **28 airlines to 80 destinations.** Airlift is not the bottleneck. [DECISION] A named airline partner (Icelandair or PLAY) is the most natural title-adjacent sponsor in this market.

### 7.2 Accommodation, this *is* the bottleneck

[FACT] The capital region had **5,620 rooms across 60 hotels** in August 2025, running **92.2% occupancy**. Plus ~4,185 active short-term rental listings. [FACT] Counter-signal: capital-region hotel stays were down 6.5% YoY in June 2026, while South (+10.9%) and North (+5.8%) grew.

Strategy: block rooms early and hold them, tiered packages (hostel to premium), camping at Reykjavík Eco-Campsite. [FACT] Accommodation tax is ISK 800/night (hotels) and ISK 400/night (campsites), rates rose 1 Jan 2025.

### 7.3 Excursions, "a few hours or a few days"

- **Half-day** (during the festival): Golden Circle express, Sky Lagoon or Blue Lagoon, whale watching, Reykjanes volcanic sites
- **Full-day** (bookended): South coast, black sand beaches, glacier hiking, and Snæfellsnes explicitly framed as a pilgrimage back to the eclipse site
- **Multi-day, 2–7 days**: Ring Road, Highlands, Westfjords, North Iceland and Mývatn
- **Signature side quests** (the Secret Solstice inheritance worth keeping): the glacier party (Langjökull), the lava tunnel concert (Raufarhólshellir), the harbour boat party, the geothermal pool party. Iceland Eclipse priced Unique Experiences at $100k and Branded Excursions at $50k.

[DECISION] Merchant of record vs. affiliate/marketplace is the biggest structural choice in the travel business, changes cash flow shape and risk.

## 8. Audience: getting to 50/50

[FACT] Iceland's population is 394,324; Reykjavík municipality 139,804. [ASSUMPTION] At 8,000/day, 50% local = ~4,000 Icelanders per day, ~2.9% of the municipality.

Local acquisition levers: price in ISK honestly, day and single-evening tickets (locals don't buy weekend passes to an event in their own city), a genuinely Icelandic bill, family/daytime programming in OPEN, Icelandic-language marketing that isn't a translation.

[ASSUMPTION] The reach figures in the 2026 partnership deck (1.5M page views, 750k uniques, 100k+ email list, 82k Facebook, 44k Instagram, 50M+ impressions) could not be independently verified, and two ratios need checking before they go in front of a sponsor: 100k+ signups from 750k uniques is a 13.3% capture rate (5–10x typical benchmarks), and 50M+ impressions against ~126k combined social following implies essentially all reach was paid or earned PR. Both may be right, but label them accurately rather than letting a sponsor read them as owned audience.

## 10. Commercial architecture (first pass)

### 10.1 Revenue lines
Festival tickets · travel packages · excursions and side quests · sponsorship · F&B and merchandise · the downtown circuit (WHITE NIGHT: wristband, upgrade, or venue-by-venue is an open decision).

### 10.2 Sponsorship, what changes from the eclipse deck
The Iceland Eclipse tier skeleton (Solar $500k / Lunar $200k / Orbital $100k / Satellite / Custom, plus Unique Experiences $100k, Branded Excursions $50k, Innovation Residency $200k) is sound. New urban inventory: airline partner, hospitality/hotel group partner, venue naming across WHITE NIGHT, the pools (FIRST LIGHT), city and destination partners (Reykjavíkurborg, Íslandsstofa/Business Iceland, Visit Reykjavík), geothermal/renewable energy.

[DECISION] Fix inherited inconsistencies from the 2026 deck: it prices Satellite at both $50,000 (summary slide) and $20,000 (detail slide), the deck's detail pages agree on $20,000 twice, use that.

### 10.3 Cost structure, the urban advantage
Deleted or shrunk vs. a remote build: temporary campsite city, water/sewage infrastructure, shuttle fleet, most of the generator load, long freight runs, artist/staff accommodation crisis. Added: city permit fees, police cost recovery past 23:30, turf protection, harder load-in.

## 11. Open decisions (selected, see original doc for full table with owners/deadlines)

1. Alcohol hours: is the park inside the extended city-centre zone (23:30) or not (22:00)? **Blocks daypart design, unresolved.**
2. Capacity: survey, egress model, agree a number with city and police.
3. WHITE NIGHT circuit: wristband, upgrade, or separate? Before pricing.
4. Icelandic artist share: proposal ≥40% of slots. Before booking.
5. Egypt Eclipse (2 Aug 2027) is 3 weeks later, cannibalization or combined product? Before either goes on sale.
6. Publish a Midnight Sun priority-access promise for 2026 alumni. Before on-sale.

**Closed since v1:** LungA is defunct, so there is no mid-July domestic clash to negotiate. Dates (9–11 July 2027) are already published on the public site and should be treated as fixed, not open.
